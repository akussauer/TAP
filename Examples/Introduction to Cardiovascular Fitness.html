What is Cardiovascular Fitness?

Lecture | Time: 00:04:00

Cardiovascular Fitness

Cardiovascular fitness refers to the ability of the heart, lungs, and blood vessels to deliver oxygen to working muscles during sustained physical activity.

Key Terms:

  • Heart Rate — beats per minute (BPM)
  • Aerobic Exercise — activity that raises your heart rate for an extended period
  • VO2 Max — the maximum rate of oxygen your body can use

Why does it matter?

  • Reduces risk of heart disease
  • Improves energy levels
  • Supports mental health
  • Increases endurance

Checking Resting Heart Rate

Demo | Time: 00:03:30

Demo: Finding Your Pulse

Before we get moving, let's establish your resting heart rate (RHR).

How to find your pulse:

  1. Place two fingers on the inside of your wrist (radial pulse) or the side of your neck (carotid pulse)
  2. Count the beats for 30 seconds
  3. Multiply by 2 to get your BPM

Average Resting Heart Rates:

Age Group Average RHR
Children (6–15) 70–100 BPM
Adults (18+) 60–100 BPM
Athletes 40–60 BPM

Write down your RHR — we'll compare it after the workout!

Interval Training Explained

Lecture | Time: 00:03:00

What is Interval Training?

Interval training alternates between periods of high-intensity effort and lower-intensity recovery.

Today's Format — HIIT (High-Intensity Interval Training):

  • Work period: 40 seconds
  • Rest period: 20 seconds
  • Rounds: 3 sets of the full circuit

Benefits:

  • Burns more calories in less time
  • Improves aerobic and anaerobic fitness
  • Keeps things interesting!

Aim to work at 70–85% of your maximum effort during work periods.

Cool-Down Stretching

Exercise | Time: 00:05:00

Cool-Down — Static Stretching (5 Minutes)

Slowly bring your heart rate down with these held stretches (30 sec each):

  1. Standing quad stretch — hold ankle, balance on one foot
  2. Hamstring stretch — seated or standing toe touch
  3. Hip flexor lunge stretch — deep lunge, back knee down
  4. Chest opener — clasp hands behind back, open chest
  5. Shoulder cross-body stretch
  6. Child's pose — hold for 60 seconds to finish

Breathe deeply through each stretch. Never bounce — hold steady.

Partner Fitness Goal Setting

Group work | Time: 00:02:30

Setting a Fitness Goal

With a partner, use the SMART goal framework to set one fitness goal for the next 2 weeks.

SMART Goals:

Letter Meaning Example
S Specific "I will run 3x per week"
M Measurable "For 20 minutes each time"
A Achievable "Starting with a walk/run mix"
R Relevant "To improve my 1-mile time"
T Time-bound "Over the next 2 weeks"

Write your goal down — you'll review it at our next session!
